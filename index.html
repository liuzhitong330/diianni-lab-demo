The Di Ianni Lab combines ultrasound sensing and intervention. If a compressed acquisition is to inform an intervention, a good-looking reconstruction is only the beginning: can strong whole-image agreement hide a bias in weaker signals?

I built a small review tool using the lab’s released Deep-fUS models and 40 public test pairs. It reproduces the published image-level results, then separates signal-dependent bias from its contribution to total error—so the next paired-reference check can target a specific discrepancy.

How it works

Each sample pairs real compounded ultrasound data with a conventional power-Doppler reference. Five models reconstruct it from 125 down to 25 real frames. Compare the same pixels across models; signal bands are defined by the reference, not by the prediction or an anatomical label.

This is a static-image reanalysis, not a new validation dataset. Power Doppler is an indirect blood-flow-related signal, not a direct neural recording. The released files do not contain the time series needed to test stimulus responses.

Why this matters for the lab

Reliable sensing matters when ultrasound measurements are used to guide intervention. This reanalysis asks a narrower question than “does the image look right?”: where does compression change the signal, and is that discrepancy substantial or merely large relative to a tiny denominator?

My proposed next check would pair short and conventional acquisitions in repeated, controlled stimulus trials. Include sham trials, motion annotations and acquisition timing; compare response amplitude and timing in predefined regions. A discrepancy that persists without motion would motivate reconstruction/calibration checks. One confined to motion or timing changes would point to acquisition controls. Neither explanation can be resolved by these static files.

Methods note

The official code, test data and models accompany Di Ianni & Airan (2022). Five pretrained networks were rerun without retraining. The conventional reconstruction is a comparator, not absolute physiological ground truth. Forty file IDs have no released animal/session mapping; no independence, anatomical segmentation or temporal-response claim is made.

Definitions, reproducibility and limits

Reference-relative signal is 10 log₁₀(reference / reference peak). Bands use lower-inclusive, upper-exclusive boundaries, except the top band includes the peak. Signed band bias is 100 × Σ(prediction − reference) / Σreference within that band; it can cancel opposite errors. The separate absolute-error contribution uses Σ|prediction − reference| divided by whole-image reference power. All quantitative metrics retain negative predictions.

125, 100, 75, 50 and 25 real frames correspond to 75, 80, 85, 90 and 95% less beamformed data relative to 250 complex frames. Different pretrained models are used at each budget: this is not a controlled causal test of adding frames, nor a claim about equal time savings. Display arrays are rounded; numerical metrics use unrounded inference output.

To reuse this on a second dataset, retain matched reference/prediction IDs, units, frame budgets and normalization. Physiological interpretation additionally needs animal/session IDs, stimulus logs, timestamps and repeated trials. The tolerance here has not been calibrated to any biological or clinical outcome.
